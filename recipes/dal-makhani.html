Dal Makhani

Dal Makhani is a rich, creamy, and buttery lentil curry originating from the Punjab region of the Indian subcontinent, widely recognized as a quintessential dish of North Indian and Punjabi cuisine. The name translates to "buttery lentils," with dal meaning lentils and makhani deriving from makhan (butter).

The dish is primarily made by slow-cooking whole black urad dal (black gram) and rajma (red kidney beans) in a spiced onion-tomato puree, then finishing it with generous amounts of butter and fresh cream. This long simmering process, traditionally done in clay pots or over dying coals, creates a luxurious, velvety texture and a deep, savory flavor profile often enhanced with aromatic spices like garam masala and kasuri methi (dried fenugreek leaves).

It is typically served as a main course or side dish with naan, roti, jeera rice, or other flatbreads. While traditionally a special occasion dish due to its richness and preparation time, modern variations allow for quicker preparation using pressure cookers or Instant Pots, though the slow-cooked version remains the gold standard for authenticity.

Ingredients

Serves 4 to 5

  • 3/4 cup whole whole urad (whole black lentil)
  • 2 tbsp rajma (kidney beans)
  • salt to taste
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds (jeera)
  • 2 green chilli , cut lengthwise
  • 25 mm stick dalchini (cinnamon)
  • 2 clove (laung / lavang)
  • 3 elaichi ( green cardamom )
  • 1/2 cup finely chopped onion
  • 1/2 tsp ginger-garlic (adrak-lehsun) paste
  • 1 tsp red chilli powder
  • 1/4 tsp haldi powder (turmeric powder)
  • 1 1/2 cups fresh tomato pulp
  • 1/2 cup cream
  • 2 tbsp chopped coriander (dhania)
  • 1 tbsp fresh cream for the garnish

Steps

  1. Wash the ¾ cup whole urad dal and 2 tbsp rajma thoroughly. Soak them together in plenty of water for 8 hours or overnight.

  2. Drain the soaked dal and rajma. Add fresh water and salt, then pressure cook for about 6–8 whistles, or until completely soft.

  3. Lightly mash the cooked dal and rajma with the back of a spoon. Keep aside.

  4. Heat 3 tbsp butter in a heavy-bottomed pan. Add 1 tsp cumin seeds.

  5. Once the cumin starts crackling, add the green chillies, cinnamon, cloves and green cardamoms. Sauté for 30–40 seconds.

  6. Add ½ cup finely chopped onion and cook until golden brown.

  7. Add ½ tsp ginger-garlic paste and sauté for 1–2 minutes.

  8. Add 1 tsp red chilli powder and ¼ tsp turmeric powder. Stir quickly for a few seconds.

  9. Add 1½ cups fresh tomato pulp and cook for 8–10 minutes, until the tomato mixture thickens and the butter starts separating.

  10. Add the cooked urad dal and rajma. Mix everything well.

  11. Add a little hot water if needed and simmer on low heat for 30–45 minutes. Stir occasionally.

  12. Add ½ cup cream and mix well. Simmer for another 3–5 minutes on low heat.

  13. Add 2 tbsp chopped coriander and adjust the salt to taste.

  14. Serve hot and garnish with 1 tbsp fresh cream.
